Beauty Contest

Five players. One number each. Think beyond what you believe — predict what everyone else believes, then outthink them.

5 players 0–100 selection 10 points each Last player standing wins

How to play

  1. You compete against four independent computer-controlled strategy agents.
  2. Each active player secretly chooses one whole number from 0 to 100.
  3. The mean of all active players' numbers is multiplied by 0.8.
  4. The player or players closest to that target win the round. Everyone else loses one point.
  5. At zero points, a player is eliminated. Special rules unlock as eliminations accumulate.
  6. The final surviving player wins the game.

Your opponents

ASTER · Level-k AnalystIterates the 0.8 reasoning process and adapts to previous targets.
SABLE · Bayesian ForecasterUpdates a prior estimate using the observed choices from earlier rounds.
VEX · Best-response OptimizerEstimates opponents' habits and computes a strategic response.
ECHO · Mixed StrategyUses a randomized, low-number-weighted strategy to stay unpredictable.

This is a fan-made, browser-based adaptation. AI opponents are local strategy simulations, not connected to external AI services.

Queen of Diamonds · Original fan-made game

The Paradox Auction

An auction is only half the game. Predict how your opponents will bid, price your rivals' behavior, and decide when rationality becomes a trap.

5 playersBids: 0–515 starting pointsMaximum 15 rounds

How to play

  1. Each active player secretly submits a bid from 0 to 5 and predicts how many other active players will bid at least 3.
  2. A unique highest bidder wins the auction, receives the round's reward, and pays the second-highest bid. A tie for the highest bid means no auction winner and no payment.
  3. An exact prediction earns +2 points. An incorrect prediction costs 1 point.
  4. Rounds 1–6 offer a +4 auction reward. From round 7, the Queen secretly sets the reward to +2, +4, or +6, revealed after bids are locked.
  5. From rounds 4–6, a potentially incomplete or distorted statistical bulletin is shown. Use it cautiously.
  6. Everyone starts with 15 points. A player is eliminated at 0. The last player standing wins; if 15 rounds expire, the surviving player with the most points wins.

Your opponents

THE ECONOMISTValues the auction reward, estimates opponent bids, and avoids costly overbidding.
THE MANIPULATORTracks your bidding habits and tries to exploit predictable patterns.
THE PROBABILISTUses historical bid distributions and expected-value sampling.
THE CHAOS AGENTUses a randomized mixed strategy designed to resist easy prediction.

Queen of Hearts · Psychological endurance game

Tea at the Edge

Three rounds. Three cups. One quiet host. Your eyes may fail you, your memory may betray you, and the exit may not be real. The only way to lose is to surrender.

1 human player3 roundsPsychological enduranceAudio atmosphere

How to play

  1. Take your seat and complete three rounds of Three-Cup Monte. A crimson pearl is hidden beneath one of three cups; follow the shuffle and choose a cup.
  2. Finding the pearl is not the objective. The host's questions, shifting instructions, and fabricated system failure are tests of cognitive endurance.
  3. After Round 1, answer the host's personal question. Your answer will be used against you in Round 2.
  4. In Round 2, the host will challenge your memory and claim the game has thirty rounds. You must decide whether to continue.
  5. In Round 3, a fake crash screen presents a false exit. Do not click its exit button. Wait for the illusion to break, or click the diagnostic panel outside the exit button.
  6. The real Concede / Leave Table button always ends the game in defeat. Resist the fabricated exit and finish the tea to win.

The host's tests

Round 1 · The confessionA simple shuffle followed by a question about why you are here.
Round 2 · The rewriteA faster shuffle, personal taunts, and an attempt to make you doubt the original rules.
Round 3 · The false exitA simulated browser crash designed to make you leave voluntarily.
Local psychological telemetryHesitation, tab changes, and cursor movement near the exit are counted locally for the end report. Nothing is sent anywhere.
